# Water bodies: flattening, containment and seams

EGMTrans keeps the ocean at 0 and every water body at one level through a datum transform, across the tiles of a run, never lets a shore post end up below the water beside it, and keeps the coast at its height above the sea. The options are `-f`/Retain Flat Areas (on by default), `-p`/Minimum Patch Size, `-c`/Minimum Containment, `-b`/Coastal Band, `-m`/Create Mask, `--context`/Neighboring Tiles, and the water-level tables.

- **Flat areas.** Every 4-connected patch of at least `-p` posts of one height (the same whole centimeter; whole meters for DTED) is a candidate water body. It counts as one when at least `-c` (default 80%) of its boundary posts lie above it in the input; ocean neighbors are neutral, so lagoons and river mouths qualify; a contour band on a slope or a flat hilltop does not qualify and is transformed post by post. In a batch run the share is summed over every part of a water body, so both sides of a seam reach the same verdict. The ocean (0 m) stays at 0. Every water body is set to the lowest of its transformed values, so no land post is changed and no shore post can end up below the water beside it. The log counts, per file, the flat areas left as terrain and how the shore posts stand to the water after the transform.
- **Enclosed low spots.** In floating-point data, a few posts below the water body beside them, bounded by the water and by higher ground, are set to the water's level and join it in the mask, when there are fewer of them than `-p`. A low spot that reaches lower water (an outlet), the tile edge or a void is left as it is, and so is every post of DTED and whole-meter input. The log counts the spots and lists every raise of a meter or more with its position.
- **The coast.** The geoid models, not the terrain, decide where a coast ends up relative to the sea: EGM2008 fits the sea where EGM96 does not, so the plain shift to EGM96 sinks a coast below the sea where EGM96 lies above it (27% of the globe; by 3 to 4 m on the Rakhine coast of Myanmar and the swamp plains of southern New Guinea) and sets it on a cliff above the sea where EGM96 lies below it. EGMTrans keeps low ground connected to the sea at its height above the sea within the geoid correction, in both directions, and blends the shift in over the `-b`/Coastal Band above that (10 m by default, widened to twice the correction where that is more; `-b 0` turns the rule off); higher ground gets the plain shift. This preserves the coastline morphology of the full-resolution source DEM and keeps the errors of the geoid undulation models, EGM96's in particular, off the shoreline, where MIL-PRF-89020B 3.5.3.2.1 asks for the land to be higher than the adjacent water. The two directions are exact inverses of each other, so a round trip restores the land. Low ground is every post below the correction plus the band that is 4-connected to the ocean, a flat area taken whole and a void passed through; a low basin that does not reach the sea (an endorheic basin, a valley behind a higher ridge) gets the plain shift. In a batch run the regions are joined across the seams, so both sides of a seam agree; a region that reaches an edge of the run with no sea anywhere in the run keeps the plain shift and is listed in the log: give the neighbors as `--context` if it is a coast. A water body in such ground takes its level under the same rule, and the water-level table records it (`coastal`), so a later run reproduces the level. In DTED output, coastal land that would round to 0 is written as 1 m (3.5.3.2.1: "extremely shallow land just interior to coastlines shall have + 1 meter elevation"). The log counts, per file, the posts that kept their height above the sea, the posts blended and the largest correction suppressed. The strip and the band are the places where the honest EGM96 height is deliberately not written; where that height matters more than the shoreline, run with `-b 0`.
- **Water bodies that span tiles.** In a batch run, patches are joined across the seams between tiles and each water body takes one level over all its parts, so the tiles edge-match. A flat area smaller than `-p` at a tile edge takes the level of the water body a neighboring tile continues it with, so the two cells agree at the seam; on its own it stays terrain. The log explains pass 1 when it starts, lists for every tile the flat areas that reach one of its edges (largest first, with the sides they touch), and ends pass 1 with two tables: the water bodies that span more than one tile, and the ones that touch an edge with no neighbor in the run, because a neighbor transformed separately may give such a body a different level. In both tables, height (in) is the body's height in the input; level (out) is its level in the output, with the whole meters written to DTED in parentheses and `[table]` when the level comes from a water-level table; tiles and posts count its parts; and every tile is named with the sides (N, S, W, E) its part touches, where `*` marks a side with no neighbor in the run, `(context)` a tile analyzed but not written, and `(finished)` a tile written by an earlier run and skipped. A seam where the two sides differ in height is noted under the body concerned. Put the tiles that share a lake or river in one run, or give the neighbors as `--context` (Neighboring Tiles in ArcGIS Pro), or pass the `--water-levels` table exported by an earlier run over the larger area; with the table, the level does not depend on the order in which the tiles are produced. With `--skip-existing` (Skip Existing Cells), the inputs of the outputs that are skipped are analyzed as context, so a resumed run gives the shared water bodies the levels an uninterrupted run would, and their seams are checked with the new outputs.
- **Neighboring tiles** (`--context FOLDER`, may be repeated) are additional tiles that constrain the input file or folder: read and analyzed with it, but never processed or written. The input's own tiles are always analyzed together; use the option when the neighbors are produced in another run. The folder is searched like the input, every tile's placement is read from its header, only the tiles that adjoin the run (directly or through other context tiles) are analyzed, and tiles that are also in the input are skipped. The tiles must be in the source datum: a finished tile in another datum, such as a delivered EGM96 cell beside an EGM2008 run, is left out with a warning, because analyzed as the source datum it would give every shared water body the wrong level.
- **Water-level tables.** `--export-water-levels FILE` writes the level of every water body that touches a tile edge, keyed by the edge crossing. `--water-levels FILE` reads such a table in a later run: a water body found in it takes the table's level when that is lower than the level found in the run. The table is a comma-separated file with decimal points; a copy re-saved by a spreadsheet with semicolons or decimal commas is refused with a message that says so (a byte order mark is fine).
- **When nothing is leveled.** With flattening off, or with WGS84 as the source or target datum (flattening applies between orthometric heights only), neighboring tiles, water-level tables, the coastal band and the mask have no effect; the run says so once and ignores them.
- **Masks.** `-m` writes a mask beside each output: 1 for the ocean and one value per water body, so it shows exactly what was flattened, DTED included. For a DTED cell made from GeoTIFF the mask is named for the source tile (`<source stem>_mask.tif`, with the cell added when one tile covers several cells) and sits beside the cell in the DTED tree, where DTED readers ignore it; every other output gets `<output stem>_mask.tif`.
- **The containment option** (`-c`) ranges from 0 to 1; 0 keeps every flat area, 1 keeps only areas whose whole boundary lies above them.
